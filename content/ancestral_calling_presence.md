The practitioner shares that their ancestors have been calling them to study herbalism for five years. The calls were persistent but the practitioner kept delaying: "I don't have time," "I don't have resources," or simply not answering. This month (October, Hoodoo Heritage Month and Gullah Geechee Heritage Month), they finally had the "space, time and resources" to begin, and are now making their first tinctures in an herbalism class. This raises important questions about ancestral calls: How long do we ignore what's calling us? What does it cost to wait for "enough" time, money, or other resources? The practitioner frames this as specifically relevant for descendants of enslaved people whose "histories have been heavily edited, erased, violently suppressed and oppressed." The timing—finally answering during heritage months honoring their specific lineage (Gullah Geechee corridor)—suggests ancestral calls have their own timing and persistence. They don't stop calling just because we're not ready. The practitioner's five-year delay wasn't refusal but capacity-building, and the ancestors apparently knew to keep calling until the conditions aligned. This pattern likely shows up with clients as well: spiritual/ancestral calls that persist despite being ignored, and the question of when someone is truly ready vs. when they're avoiding the discomfort of answering.
