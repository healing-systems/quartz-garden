The practitioner experienced a creative awakening through their first collaboration on a poetry project, which revealed how thoroughly they'd abandoned poetry—a practice they identify as central to their life and healing. The neglect wasn't intentional but accumulated through burnout, competing obligations, and the pressure of running multiple businesses.

The collaboration involved pushing beyond their usual style (trying prose poems), showing work publicly for the first time, and working with another artist. This experience was "scary" but gave them "renewed sense of energy and passion." It highlighted that creative expression isn't extra or optional—it's medicine and a form of wholeness.

This connects to a broader pattern the practitioner is noticing: allowing urgent business tasks and obligations to crowd out what actually sustains them. They're now recommitting to their DIY MFA program, regular journal submissions (previously 1-2 monthly), and intentional writing/reading practice.

The key insight: **creative intuition isn't what you do after everything else is done—it's how you stay whole.** Treating it as optional or extra is itself a form of bypassing the self. The practitioner is reframing creative work not as indulgence but as necessary spiritual practice.
