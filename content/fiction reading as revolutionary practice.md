The art of finding well-crafted worlds to disappear into is disappearing in American society, and this loss isn't accidental. Fiction reading—real, immersive, imagination-activating reading—serves as both medicine and resistance during heavy collective moments. Fiction isn't escapism; it's survival. It practices the muscle of believing in different possibilities. It offers hope and respite when the world feels overwhelming. The decline of reading culture correlates with increased isolation and decreased imagination about alternative ways of being. Current observation: Getting back into reading fiction feels radical right now, almost countercultural. There's a sense that if Americans read more—really got lost in stories, exercised imagination—we'd be better equipped to envision and create different futures. The ability to imagine other worlds strengthens the ability to transform this one.
