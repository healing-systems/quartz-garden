From Nagoski's Burnout
The body activates fight/flight/freeze responses to threats but often doesn't complete the biological cycle it initiated. Modern life forces us to "move on" without finishing the action our nervous system prepared for - we never actually ran, fought, or allowed ourselves true stillness. This creates trapped activation that contributes to burnout and nervous system dysregulation. Completion requires following the body's actual impulse rather than doing "opposite action" to return to window of tolerance. If hyperaroused (fight/flight), the body needs vigorous movement - dancing, running, shaking - to discharge the energy. If frozen, the body needs to honor shutdown through weighted blankets, child's pose, fetal position, or corpse pose. This approach differs from traditional grounding techniques that fight against the body's current state. This framework challenges wellness culture's emphasis on "calming down quickly" and reframes nervous system work as completion rather than suppression. It's particularly relevant for clients carrying collective trauma and constant activation from societal violence.
