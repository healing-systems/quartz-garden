The practitioner is confronting a significant tenson: they are building Sweet Bay Healing as a space for free community gathering while identifying as an introvert who finds group connection exhausting. They describe loving people but not liking people, needing significant effort to figure out social engagement, being energized by one-on-one rather than group interaction, and likely experiencing social anxiety. Despite recognizing they might not be "the best person" to create communal spaces, they see the need for free gathering places where practitioners and seekers can connect non-transactionally. This creates an interesting hypothesis: perhaps those who struggle most with connection are exactly who should build spaces for it, because they understand the cost, the effort required, and what it truly means to show up. This reflects a broader pattern in the practice of working from woundedness rather than from mastery—building what you yourself need, even (especially) when it's hard for you. The practitioner is not waiting to be an extroverted natural community builder before creating community infrastructure.
